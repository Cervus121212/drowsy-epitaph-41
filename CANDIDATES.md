# Candidates — Famous Last Words: The Funniest Epitaph

Field at build time: 44 submissions. Strongest visible rivals: "I said the
beam would hold. The beam let go.", "I died as I lived: on hold with customer
support", "He Outlived Every Subscription", "Remind me later", "Do Not
Resuscitate. Do Not Reply All.", "I said I'd be back. I lied.", "I said
'watch this.' Now I'm the warning.", "If you're reading this, the Wi-Fi
password dies with me.", "I hit snooze one last time. This one stuck.",
"Still Billing", "The Autopsy Disagreed", "He Took the Password With Him".

Scored against the judge's criteria in order: (1) one-line epitaph of famous
last words; (2) real laugh from a genuine joke, not a mild pun or sad
sentiment; (3) surprise/twist/double meaning landing cleanly; (4) reads
carved in stone, short, no stage direction/explanation; (5) quotable.

Crowded lanes avoided: passwords/Wi-Fi, subscriptions/billing, snooze,
"watch this", "I said X", customer support, "died as I lived".

## CHOSEN

### "I asked for more time. They gave me a deadline." — 47 chars

- (1) First-person dying speech, one line. Pass.
- (2) Real joke: ironic reversal, ask for more time and receive a deadline.
  Not a mild pun, not sad.
- (3) Double meaning of "deadline" lands in the last word, cleanly.
- (4) 47 chars, two short sentences, nothing to explain.
- (5) "They gave me a deadline" repeats well.
- Beats beam (narrative irony, no twist word) and customer support (formula
  + complaint, no twist) on criteria 2-3; beats subscription (third person)
  and "Remind me later" (a UI label, not speech) on criterion 1.

## REJECTED

### "I died doing what I loved: nothing." — 35 chars

Sharp punchline, but "he loved nothing" reads as sad/nihilistic sentiment
(criterion 2 warns against sad sentiment), and it lives in the same
"died as I..." formula family as the field's strongest rival (customer
support), so it would likely lose that head-to-head. A collapse, not a twist
with a double meaning.

### "I finally got the last word. It was 'whatever'." — 47 chars

Setup is a cliché, punchline is a shrug rather than a clean twist. The laugh
is a smile, smaller than the deadline reversal. No double meaning.

### "I take it back. All of it. Especially Tuesday." — 46 chars

Charming specificity ("Especially Tuesday") but needs a beat to land: the
reader must supply what "it" is. Weaker and less immediate than the deadline
reversal; the joke is a tease, not a twist.

### "I donated my body to science. It was returned." — 46 chars

Known joke shape (body returned like a defective product), less original.
Will-statement voice is weaker dying speech than a direct last-words line,
and "returned" does the work of a punchline without a double meaning.

### "I told the doctor it was just a cough." — 38 chars

Relatable, but the laugh is recognition, not a twist; no double meaning.
Adjacent to the famous real epitaph "I told you I was ill", so it risks
feeling derivative. No surprise.
